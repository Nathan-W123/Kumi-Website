Where your code lives

Your code does not stay on your own machine, and it is not held in a cloud account of yours. Nothing is pushed back to GitHub unless you ask for it: import is a clone, and export is an explicit one-way push onto a branch of its own.

Your organisation's data sits on the same deployment as other customers', separated by the access control in the software rather than by separate machines. A self-hosted deployment, where the machine would be yours, is not open yet.

What happens to your data when things end

After your account or your organisation is terminated, by you or by us, we will keep your data for 30 days and then delete it. The window is there so you can ask for a copy or change your mind. We should be plain about how this works today: KUMI is pre-release and has no automated deletion, so this is a commitment we carry out by hand when you ask us to close the account — not something the software does on a timer.

Two more things you should know. There is no packaged export today. You can push a repository to a GitHub remote of your own, and you can read your audit timeline in the app. For anything else — your threads, or one copy of the whole record — write to kumi.support@gmail.com and we will get it to you by hand. And KUMI keeps an append-only, hash-chained audit log, so that an entry cannot be changed or removed without the record showing it — the database itself refuses an update, and refuses a deletion unless an operator checkpoint already covers the rows. Be plain about the limit: that makes tampering visible, not impossible, because anyone with write access to the database could recompute the chain. Entries in that log that refer to a user are not removed by the 30-day deletion, and we keep it as the integrity record of the system.

Deleting a repository inside KUMI removes our copy of the repository and the work records tied to it. Not everything KUMI made from that repository goes at the same moment — working copies made for editing, and images posted into a channel, are covered by the account deletion above rather than by deleting the repository.
